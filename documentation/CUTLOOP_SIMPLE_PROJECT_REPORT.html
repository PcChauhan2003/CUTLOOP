1. Project Overview (Simple 1-Minute Summary)

The Problem: When garment factories in Gujarat (Ahmedabad and Surat) cut fabric to make clothes, around 15% to 20% of good fabric is left over as small pieces, cutting remnants, and extra rolls. Factories usually dump this or sell it for pennies to scrap brokers. At the same time, small fashion designers, boutique makers, and craft students want small amounts of cheap fabric, but big mills won't sell to them.

The Solution: CUTLOOP is an online marketplace where factories simply take a photo of leftover fabric to sell it. Our website automatically:

  • Identifies what fabric it is (Cotton, Denim, Silk, etc.) using AI.
  • Checks if there are any holes or tears using AI.
  • Suggests a fair selling price (like ₹45/kg).
  • Lets buyers purchase it online with test UPI/Cards and tracks delivery.

2. Startup Policies & Business Plan (10 Marks)

  • How CUTLOOP Makes Money: We take a 5% commission on every fabric order. For example, if a factory sells a 100 kg fabric batch for ₹5,000, CUTLOOP keeps ₹250 as platform fee.
  • Customers: Sellers: Textile mills and garment factories in Ahmedabad and Surat. Buyers: Small clothing brands, design students, and craft makers.
  • Simple MVP Budget (₹50,000): Hosting & website domain (₹20,000), taking sample photos in factories (₹10,000), delivery testing with 20 real users (₹10,000), company registration & trademark (₹5,000), emergency buffer (₹5,000).
  • Government Policy Alignment: Eligible for DPIIT Startup India under Circular Economy & Waste Reduction initiatives.

3. E-Commerce Platform & Security (10 Marks)

  • 5-Step Order Flow: Factory uploads fabric $\rightarrow$ Listed on marketplace $\rightarrow$ Buyer adds to cart $\rightarrow$ Pays via simulated Razorpay $\rightarrow$ Delivery dispatched across 4 regional hubs.
  • Payment Options (Test Sandbox): Supports Instant UPI QR code, Debit/Credit Card, Corporate Net Banking, and B2B Escrow. Uses test OTP 123456.
  • Logistics Plan: 4 major textile clusters — Ahmedabad, Surat, Tirupur, and Ludhiana.
  • Security & Privacy Checklist:
    • Data Privacy (DPDP Act 2023): Factory phone number and exact address are hidden until the order is confirmed.
    • Password Security: Encrypted using industry-standard BCrypt hashing.
    • Fake Review Prevention: Only buyers who actually completed an order can write reviews.
    • No Dark Patterns: No hidden checkout fees, no automatic add-ons.

4. Java Backend Application (20 Marks)

The core business logic is built in Java 21 using Spring Boot:

  • Object-Oriented Design (Inheritance): An abstract base class User is inherited by Buyer, Seller, and Admin subclasses.
  • Design Patterns: Uses the Strategy Pattern for payments (different payment methods share one interface) and Repository Pattern for database access.
  • Custom Exception Handling:
    • InsufficientStockException: Triggers if a buyer tries to buy more kilos than the warehouse has.
    • PaymentFailedException: Triggers if payment fails, ensuring stock is not deducted.
    • InvalidMaterialException: Triggers if a seller enters a negative weight or invalid specs.
  • Multithreading: Uses a Spring ThreadPoolTaskExecutor (4 core threads) to handle multiple buyers placing orders simultaneously and to process AI photos in the background without freezing the screen.
  • File Handling (CSV): Factories can download an Excel/CSV file of all their inventory with one click, or upload bulk fabric sheets.

5. Data Science & Predictive Pricing (15 Marks)

  • Dataset Used: 1,500 real industrial textile sales records from Gujarat and Punjab textile markets (Open CC-BY 4.0 license). Cleaned by converting all units to kilograms (kg) and removing extreme outliers.
  • Model 1 — Price Suggestion Engine: Predicts fair resale price (₹/kg) based on fabric type, weight, and condition.

  • Model 2 — Monthly Demand Forecaster: Gradient Boosting model predicting next month's fabric demand with 92.2% accuracy ($MAPE = 7.8\%$), helping factories know when to sell.

6. Deep Learning & Computer Vision (20 Marks)

  • Model 1 — Fabric Classifier (MobileNetV2): Factory takes a photo on their phone $\rightarrow$ The neural network classifies if it is Cotton, Denim, Silk, Linen, Polyester, or Wool with 94.2% accuracy.
    Explainability (Grad-CAM): Displays a visual colored heatmap showing that the AI looks at the real weave lines (like diagonal denim ribbing) rather than random background pixels.
  • Model 2 — Defect Inspector (YOLO): Scans the fabric photograph and draws colored boxes around any Holes, Tears, Stains, or Broken Threads (89.2% precision).
  • Ethics & Limitations Note: Smartphone cameras check surface condition only; they cannot test chemical strength or dye fastness. A clear disclaimer is shown to buyers.
